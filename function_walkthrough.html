Part 1: from 21 landmarks to 42 comparable numbers

The same hand gesture can appear at different places and sizes in the camera image. The function removes the position (by measuring from the wrist) and then the size (by dividing by the palm length). Part 2: from 42 numbers to a gesture label

    
The classifier is a set of hand-written rules. It compares two distances against two thresholds: the gap between the thumb tip and the index tip (a pinch is a small gap), and the distance from the wrist to the middle fingertip (an open hand has a long reach). You can step through predict, choose an example hand, drag the three circled fingertips, and change the thresholds to see when the decision flips.

Part 3: from a shaky label to one confirmed gesture

    
The camera sends about 30 pictures (frames) per second, and Part 2 labels every one of them. A real hand wobbles, so the labels can flicker: pinch, pinch, open, pinch. If every label triggered an action, the computer would react wildly. The Debouncer adds two safety rules. Hold: the same gesture must appear in a row for hold_frames frames. Cooldown: after one gesture is confirmed, everything is ignored for cooldown_seconds. In the finished program each frame will flow through extract_features, then predict, then update. Here, the labels below are a scripted example, and time is simulated at 30 frames per second.
