Part 1: from 21 landmarks to 42 comparable numbers

The same hand gesture can appear at different places and sizes in the camera image. The function removes the position (by measuring from the wrist) and then the size (by dividing by the palm length). Part 2: from 42 numbers to a gesture label

    
The classifier is a set of hand-written rules. It compares two distances against two thresholds: the gap between the thumb tip and the index tip (a pinch is a small gap), and the distance from the wrist to the middle fingertip (an open hand has a long reach). You can step through predict, choose an example hand, drag the three circled fingertips, and change the thresholds to see when the decision flips.

Part 3: the Debouncer, one line at a time

    
The classifier gives one label per camera frame, and labels can flicker. The Debouncer turns that noisy stream into a single confirmed gesture using two rules: the same gesture must appear hold_frames times in a row, and after a confirmation everything is ignored for cooldown_seconds (the break). Here, each step runs exactly one line of update. The highlighted line on the left is the line being executed, the memory cards show every value the Debouncer holds, and the table at the bottom records what came out of each frame. To keep the example short, hold_frames is 3, and one frame arrives every 0.5 s (a real camera is about 0.03 s).

Part 4: from a confirmed gesture to a real key press

    
So far the program only decides. The Debouncer says "pinch was confirmed", but nothing on the computer has moved. ActionRunner is the hand that does something. It takes an action name (a text such as "next_slide") and uses the pyautogui library to press the matching key, exactly as if you had typed it. It also adds three safety features: a master on/off switch (actions_enabled), the fail-safe (throw the mouse into the top-left corner to stop everything), and a warning for unknown action names. Choose a scenario, then step through it. Each step runs one line; on the right you see your "computer" react.

Part 5: main.py, a hand appears in front of the camera

    
main.py is the manager: it builds one object from every module, then repeats the same pipeline for every camera frame. In this simulation you are the person in front of the camera, and every step runs one line of the real main.py. The row of boxes shows the data moving through the pipeline, the dark window shows exactly what cv2.imshow would display, and the memory cards show the variables. I have not seen your config.py, so the demo uses these assumed values: HOLD_FRAMES = 2, COOLDOWN_SECONDS = 1.5, MIN_CONFIDENCE = 0.5, ACTION_MAP = {"pinch": "next_slide"}, toggle key t, quit key q, thresholds 0.4 and 1.3. One frame arrives every 0.5 s to keep it short. The story: no hand, relaxed hand, a weak pinch, a real pinch held for two frames, then you press t, then you open your hand and press q.
